Is blocking allowed?

Only one kind. Set a screen, keep your arms in, and make the defender go around.

CCCraig Clayton · Author of Girls Flag Football, Illustrated3 min read2026–27 NFHS rules

The short answer
Only a screen.

The only legal block in flag football is a screen block. The blocker gets into the defender's path first and doesn't start contact. Any other block is illegal blocking, a 10-yard penalty, signal 34. 9-3-1

Screen
01

What a screen block is

A screen block is taking a spot in an opponent's path without touching her with any part of your body. Think of a screen in basketball. 2-3-2

Every other kind of block is illegal, for every player on the field. 9-3-1

Set
02

How to set a legal screen

  • 1Get there first. The blocker has to be in the defender's path before the defender arrives. 9-3-2
  • 2Move with her, not into her. Once set, the blocker may move only in the same direction and path as the defender. 9-3-2
  • 3Arms in. No extended arms, and no hands, elbows or legs to start contact. 9-3-2

The defender gets a fair chance to go around, usually one or two steps. Stepping in front of her at the last second, or from her blind side, takes that chance away. Once a legal screen is set, it's up to the defender to avoid it.

34
03

The penalty

Illegal blocking is 10 yards. The referee shows signal 34. 9-3 PENALTY

Signal 34

Illegal block

Any block that isn't a legal screen, or a screen that starts contact.

10YARDS
“Once set, move only in the defender's direction and path. Keep arms in.”
